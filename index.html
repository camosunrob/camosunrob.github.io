Teaching Philosophy

My teaching starts with the belief that students learn best when ideas feel connected to real choices, real workplaces, and real responsibilities. I aim to make business education practical without making it narrow: students should leave with tools they can use now and habits of thinking that will continue serving them as technology, organizations, and communities change.

In the classroom, that means combining clear explanations, applied examples, reflection, discussion, and hands-on activities. I want students to practice asking better questions, weighing evidence, considering people affected by decisions, and communicating their thinking in ways others can understand.

I also design learning with accessibility and belonging in mind. Universal Design for Learning encourages me to give students multiple ways to engage, participate, and show what they know. PRME principles remind me that business education should prepare students to make responsible, ethical, and sustainable contributions beyond the classroom.

Clear Thinking

Students need more than correct answers. They need practice organizing information, identifying assumptions, explaining trade-offs, and using evidence to support sound decisions.

2

Ethical Action

Business decisions affect people. I encourage students to consider fairness, responsibility, sustainability, and the wider impact of the systems they help design, manage, or improve.

3

Inclusive Learning

Students arrive with different experiences, strengths, and constraints. I try to create courses where expectations are transparent, support is visible, and participation can take more than one meaningful form.

Experience and Approach

My work as an instructor is grounded in helping students connect concepts to the business contexts they are likely to encounter: teams, customers, data, digital tools, processes, change, and organizational goals.

  • Applied business learning

    I use examples, scenarios, and activities that help students translate course ideas into workplace-ready judgment and communication.

  • Student-centred course design

    I structure courses with clear outcomes, purposeful assessments, practical feedback, and room for students to build confidence over time.

  • Responsible use of technology

    I encourage students to understand digital tools critically, including how technology can support better work and where human judgment still matters.

What I Hope Students Carry Forward

I hope students leave my courses feeling more capable, more curious, and more prepared to contribute. Business education should help people do good work with skill and integrity, and it should help them imagine the kind of organizations and communities they want to help build.
